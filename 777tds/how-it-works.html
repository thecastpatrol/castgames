How It Works

Three modes. One target: 777 career touchdowns.

The Idea

Draft an NFL offence from every touchdown scorer since 1999, and the Legends before them. Every player carries his career scored touchdowns, but you do not see the number until you pick him. Add up your team and try to reach 777.

The Team

You fill 10 spots on the field: a quarterback, two running backs, four wide receivers, a tight end and two FLEX spots. A FLEX spot takes a running back, a wide receiver or a tight end. If a player fits more than one open spot, you choose where he goes.

Two Point

A quick-fire mode of its own: you see one player’s career TDs and call whether the next player’s number is over or under it, before the clock runs out. Every right call adds one to your streak. One wrong call and you’re out.

Wildcards

  • Reroll (Respin in Team Spin): bin the current offer and get a fresh one.
  • Reveal TDs: show the touchdowns for everyone on the current offer only.
  • Remove Player: take one player out of your team. His spot reopens and he goes back in the pool. If your team is full but short of the target, you can still use it before you lock in.

Wildcards come with a free CAST Profile: one of each per game, or two of each with CAST Pass. Guests see them locked until they sign in. Exactly 777 is Hard Mode: no free wildcards for anyone, only paid ones, one of each a game.

Legends

Now and then in 777+, one offer is a Legends Pick: three of the greats whose careers were mostly before 1999, like Jerry Rice, Emmitt Smith and Walter Payton. You get one at most per game, and a player who has fallen well behind late in a game is sometimes thrown one as a lifeline.

Legends keep their touchdowns hidden like everyone else, and they count their whole careers. They only appear in 777+.

The Rules

  • Every player can only be picked once.
  • Picks are final, unless you use Remove Player.
  • Your running total and how far you are from 777 are always on screen.
  • Offers are random, but players with more touchdowns turn up a little more often.
  • Your result shows the luck of your draw: how many touchdowns were on offer, against an average game.
  • Team colours show the team each player last played for.
  • No bonuses, no chemistry, no tricks. Just touchdowns.

Where the Numbers Come From

Every touchdown a player scored himself in the NFL regular season counts, over his whole career: rushing, receiving, kick and punt returns, and interception and fumble returns. Passing touchdowns never count, so a quarterback scores only the ones he ran in (or caught). Playoff games are not included.

The pool is every quarterback, running back, wide receiver and tight end with 5 or more scored touchdowns who played since 1999, plus 30 Legends. Seasons from 1999 on come from nflverse player stats (CC BY 4.0), checked against Wikipedia; seasons before 1999 and the Legends’ careers come from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0).

How It Works

Four modes. Two targets. 1000 for the NRL, 250 for the NRLW.Five modes. Three targets. 1000 for the NRL, 250 for the NRLW, 200 for World Cup Test tries.

The Idea

Draft a starting 13 of NRL players. Every player carries their NRL tries, but you do not see the number until you pick them. Add up your 13 and try to reach 1000.

The Team

You fill a standard starting 13: FB, W, C, C, W, FE, HB, P, HK, P, SR, SR, LK. Players can go into any position they are listed for, so a centre who also played wing can fill either spot. If a player fits more than one open spot, you choose where they go.

In Team Spin you choose the position first, before the club and season are revealed, so you are locked in. If nobody from the squad you land on plays that position, the game spins again for you.

Wildcards

  • Reroll (Respin in Team Spin): bin the current offer and get a fresh one.
  • Reveal Tries: show the tries for everyone on the current offer only.
  • Remove Player: take one player out of your 13. Their spot reopens and they go back in the pool. If your 13 is full but short of the target, you can still use it before you lock in.

Wildcards come with a free CAST Profile: one of each per game, or two of each with CAST Pass. Guests see them locked until they sign in. A free CAST Profile also adds an extra reroll every game. Exactly 1000 is Hard Mode: no free wildcards for anyone, CAST Pass included, but you can use one of each a game from your Vault or buy it on the spot.

Legends and Immortals

Now and then in 1000+, one offer is a Legends Pick: three greats of the game, most of them from before the NRL era. Rarer still is the Immortal Pick: three of Rugby League’s Immortals. You get one at most per game, and a player who has fallen well behind late in a game is sometimes thrown one as a lifeline.

Legends and Immortals keep their tries hidden like everyone else, and they count their whole premiership careers (NSWRL, ARL, Super League and NRL). They only appear in 1000+.

The Rules

  • Every player can only be picked once.
  • Picks are final, unless you use Remove Player.
  • Your running total and how far you are from 1000 are always on screen.
  • Offers are random, but players with more NRL tries turn up a little more often.
  • Your result shows the luck of your draw: how many tries were on offer, against an average game.
  • Club colours show the club each player last played for.
  • No bonuses, no chemistry, no tricks. Just tries.

Where the Numbers Come From

Tries count NRL premiership games from 1998 to now. The 1997 Super League and ARL seasons are not included, and neither are Origin, Tests or other representative games. Legends and Immortals in 1000+ are the one exception: they count their whole premiership careers. The player list matches the 27-0 player pool (apart from a handful of greats who now appear only as Legends), and only players with a confirmed try count are in the game, including players who never scored.

NRLW 250+ counts NRLW premiership tries from 2018 to now, including finals. Women’s Origin, Tests, All Stars and one-off exhibition games are not included. NRLW data comes from Rugby League Project, with the top tryscorers checked against official club pages. Players who have only ever played off the bench take the position they have played in other competitions.

World Cup 200+ counts senior Test tries for the ten 2026 World Cup nations (Australia, New Zealand, England, France, Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Lebanon and the Cook Islands), plus Great Britain, Scotland and Italy, from Rugby League Project. During the 2026 World Cup, each game’s tries and Test appearances are added once it finishes. World Legends, the international greats, sit out the normal offers: one can turn up in an offer, and a World Legend Pick lets you choose one, with his Test tries.
